# Glossary

The code is in English; the app speaks Italian to its users (`packages/phone/src/texts/`), and the project's decisions are written in Italian. This table keeps the two vocabularies aligned. A new domain term enters here before it enters the code.

The terms of the architecture paper, which is in Italian, are in [Architecture terms](#architecture-terms); [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) uses them.

The relay's words (part 8.1, MBX-1..MBX-4, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): a **deposit** is an envelope left on a deposit address D, with the time the relay received it; the relay **discards** it seven days after, collected or not. Whoever deposits is a **depositor**, anyone; whoever shows the collect secret R of the mailbox at D = hash(R) is its **holder**, and collects. The relay's domain is `mailbox/deposits`, its personas `desk/depositor` and `desk/holder`. Rejected: `box` (a second word for the mailbox), `sender` (the relay knows no sender, F2, and a depositor need not be one). A day after its discard the relay **forgets** a deposit (part 19, MBX-8, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): it keeps nothing of it, neither its row nor its facts, so that a retry of its deposit is a new deposit; rejected: _expire_ (said of a ticket, part 11.2), _purge_ (an operator's act, not the relay's own rule).

The secrets' words (part 9.1, SEA-1..SEA-7, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): the **safe** is the domain of `seals` that keeps every secret of the node: the **seeds** (32 random bytes, with their **seed scheme**, `slip10-flat`, and the **next index** a face of the seed will take), the **faces** (each at its **index** under its seed, `m/i'`, with its **private key**, its **chain code** and its **public key**, the only one that leaves) and the **mailbox keys** (a collect secret R with its deposit address D, as the key and the address of a post-office box). A **content** is what `post` gives to sign or to seal, bytes opaque to the safe; a **sealed content** is one sealed for a public key; a signature **holds** when it is the face's over that content. A content is **not sealed** for bytes that are no public key one can seal for (not a point of Ed25519's group, or a point of small order: libsodium refuses them, part 18), as a sealed content is **not opened** by a face it was not sealed for. The **primitives port** is the safe's machine: the randomness, HMAC-SHA512, Ed25519, the sealed box, BLAKE2b (libsodium, step 18). Rejected: `keys` (a second word for the faces' keys and the mailboxes'), `keyring`, `vault` (Alessandro's notes are the vault).

The bonds' words (part 11.1, BND-1..BND-6, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): the **address book** is the domain of `bonds` that keeps the node's user's **faces** and **bonds**, as an address book keeps one's own cards and one's contacts. A face has its **name** (the one it is **introduced as** to whoever it is shown), its **position** in the user's tree of faces (**at the top**, or **under a face**: data of the user's, never of the keys) and its public key, once the safe made it. A bond is one of my faces with **their face** (its key and the name it is introduced as) and **their mailbox** (its deposit address and its **relays**, the **relay addresses** where it is deposited on), **my mailbox** for them (the key's reference in the safe, its D and its relays), **the name I give** them, and the node's annotations: the **handshake**, **in person** or **at a distance**, and when; the **confirmation**, **awaited** until a letter of theirs comes on the bond ("does she have you?"), then **received**. A bond is **kept** in the list until the user **removes** it. Rejected: `contacts` (the screens' word, not the domain's: a bond is the relation, not the person), `peer` in the components (the architecture's word for a remote node), `directory` (an emergence of the network's, a shared index).

The words of the handshake (part 11.2, BND-7..BND-12, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): a bond begins as a **ticket**, asked for by the user for one of her faces: **being prepared** while the safe draws the key of the bond's mailbox, **ready** once it has its D, shown in a QR or sent after `#` in a link, then **used**, when the bond is kept, or **expired**, 48 hours after it was asked for. The **ticket's secret** is the face's signature over the ticket's mailbox address: whoever has the ticket has it, no relay can forge it. To **open their ticket** is to bond with it, presenting a ticket of mine: **my introduction** goes to their ticket's mailbox; **their introduction** comes to mine. **Crossed tickets** (the **crossed QR**) are two tickets each side opened of the other's within **the window of a crossing** (_finestra di un incrocio_, [Q, C, provisional: A to confirm]): two minutes between the time their introduction declares and my opening of their ticket (BND-10): the handshake **in person**, an annotation, not a guarantee. The **first face** is made at the phone's **first start**. **The mailboxes I collect** are my ready tickets' and my kept bonds'. Rejected: `invitation` (a ticket invites no one in particular, and is shown to whoever is in front), `invitee` for the persona (the paper's `unknown`, PER-11), `pass`, `code` for the secret (it is a signature, not a number to type).

The words of the browser's I/O (part 23, WEB-1..WEB-5, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **the ticket's link** is the address of the phone's page with a ready ticket after `#`, so that it never reaches the server that serves the page; **the ticket's QR** draws that link, so that a camera outside Cumino opens it too. To **scan** is to look at QRs with the **camera**, which is **on**, or **refused** by the user or the browser, **missing**, or **busy** (another app has it). The **front camera** faces the user, from the screen's side, the **rear camera** away from her (the browser's `user` and `environment`); **the device's cameras** are one, or more than one, another to turn to (A, 23 Nov 2026). To **save a file** gives the user a file to keep (an export); to **pick a file** takes one the user chooses (an import), or nothing. The **device's clock** is the instant its runtime reads, in UTC as it writes it, with the zone the configuration gives; the **device's network** is what the browser tells of its connectivity, **offline** for sure, or **not told** (a network says nothing of the relay; A, 23 Nov 2026); an **id drawn at random** is 16 bytes of the device's cryptographic randomness, in base64url, for the ids a node names before the first attempt (DOM-12). A **view** is a screen of the way as an address names it, a place, never a step of a flow; **a view's link** is the phone's page with the view after `#/`, in the words of her language (`#/contatti/<bond>`), told apart from a ticket's link by its mark; **the way in the history** is each screen of the way an entry of the page's session history, so that a refresh opens the same view and back goes to the one before (WEB-25; A, 24 Nov 2026: "ogni vista [...] la sua url"). Rejected: `invitation link` (a ticket invites no one in particular, as above), `upload` and `download` (no server: the file stays on the device), `UUID` for the ids (a second alphabet beside base64url's).

The post's words (part 10.1, PST-1..PST-9, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): a **letter** is a request or a response (F7), with its **declared time**, the sender's claim (PER-13); the **signed content** is what the sender's face signs, the letter with the deposit address it is for, so that a letter **re-addressed** to another mailbox does not hold there; an **envelope** is the sealed letter with its format, what a relay keeps; an **opened envelope** gives the letter and its `Caller`, or why not. **The requests** (`post/requests`) are the port of requests' record: each one **sent**, **sent again** while it waits within **its time**, and **closed**, **answered** by its response or with **no response**. **The post's machine** is what the safe does for the post, with a face of the node's user: sign, seal, open, check a signature, hash (GR-5). The **mailbox port** is how the post deposits an envelope at a relay, which **keeps** it, keeps one of that deposit **already** (an earlier attempt), refuses it, or does not answer; each deposit has its **operation**, the hash of what is deposited (a sending, a response) and its D, the same for the same deposit tried again (part 10.2, PST-10, PST-11). Rejected: `outbox` for the requests (the library's word, of every effect), `timeout` (the paper's, a transient failure; here a meaning, V18), `registered mail` (a second word for a request).

The words of auth and the personas (part 13.1, PER-22..PER-24, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **the acquaintances** (`auth/acquaintances`, _le conoscenze_) is auth's office: the register of whom the node knows, at which of its mailboxes, and what it offers them, read from the address book and the office of the extensions by its adapters (GR-4), never decided there (PER-9). Who asks (the `Caller`, PER-4) is **the user** of the node, from the app, **a face**, by the key whose signature holds, or **anonymous**. To **introduce myself** is the leaf of `unknown`, the introduction as the face that presents a ticket says it. Rejected: `contacts` and `bonds` for auth's office (the address book's words, GR-4: auth keeps its own language), `register` (a verb in the code), `peers` (the architecture's word for a remote node, not a relation).

The words of ping pong (part 14.1, PNG-1..PNG-6, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **ping pong** (`ping_pong`) is the first extension, and **the pings** (`business/pings`, _i ping_) its office, which **sends a ping** to a bond, **asks** the bond's node **for a pong** (the request, F7), and **closes the ping** with what came back: **the pong came back**, **the ping had no response** within its time, or **the peer cannot answer** it (its node closed the request or refused it, or its office gave no pong); on the served side it **answers a ping** with a **pong**, which the leaf `ping` on `known` projects as its response. Each closing is a happening of its own (`a_pong_has_been_received`, `a_ping_has_had_no_response`, `a_ping_could_not_be_answered`, DOM-6). Rejected: `ping_pong` for the office (the extension's name, not what its office keeps), `echo` (a second word for the pong), `heartbeat` and `presence` (a ping tells that a bond's node answered once, nothing it keeps), `latency` (a measure, not a happening).

The words of messaging (part M1, MSG-1..MSG-16, Claude, 26 Nov 2026; the Italian provisional): **messaging** (`messaging`, _Messaggi_) is the first extension after ping pong, and **the chats** (`business/chats`, _le chat_) its office. A bond **has its chat** (_la chat di un legame_): it is not created, its identity is the bond's. The office **writes a message** on a bond and **delivers** it (`deliver_a_message`, F4's "deliver M on bond L": the request), then **closes the message** with what came back: **the message was received**, so it is **delivered** (_consegnato_) and the response is its **receipt** (_ricevuta_); it had **no receipt** within its wait (_nessuna ricevuta_, never "not received": MSG-12); **the peer cannot receive it** (_non consegnabile_); or it was **called off** (_annullato_). On the served side it **receives a message**, which the leaf `message` on `known` answers with `received`. A message received carries its **annotations**: signature valid and bond known (the persona `known` itself), the mailbox of the bond, the time the letter **declares** (_dichiarato alle_, a claim) and the time the node **received** it (_ricevuto alle_, its own clock). **Reading a chat** up to a message leaves the ones after it **to read** (_da leggere_). **A chat at a glance** (`a_chat_at_a_glance`, _la chat in breve_, part M2) is its last message and how many are to read, for the row of a contact; the office keeps each chat in its **folder** (_cartella_), where it **files** each message at its **place** (_posto_), one after another (MSG-5). A message is **on its way** (_in uscita_) until it closes; its delivery a dead letter, it is **held** (_trattenuto_), and **put aside** (_messo da parte_) once she discards it; **send it again** (_Invia di nuovo_) writes its text as a new message. The row of a contact in **her contacts** (`a_bond_in_my_contacts`) is a place of the kernel's screens, where the chat shows its last message and what is to read. Rejected: `conversations` (a record of its own for what the bond is), `inbox` and `outbox` (the library's word for every effect), `sent` for a message written (the post sends, the office writes), `read` for the receipt (no read receipts, A, 4 Nov 2026), `ack` (a technical word for the receipt), `undelivered` (a state where "no receipt" is what happened). Its part's words (part M5, MSG-7, Claude, 27 Nov 2026): **the line** (_la riga della chat_) is the chat in its place, on a contact's row and under the slot's head: its last message and how many are **to read** (_da leggere_); **the conversation** (_la conversazione_) is the chat on the whole screen, its messages and **the field** (_il campo_) with «Invia»; **the annotations** (_le annotazioni_) of a message are what her node tells of it at a tap; **written again** («Invia di nuovo») is a message not delivered, or called off, written as a message of its own. Rejected: `bubble` as a domain word (the look's, `data-part`), `thread` (a chat is the bond's, not a thread of it).

The user's words (part 15.1, USR-1..USR-8, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **the activities** (`user/activities`, _le attività_) is the user domain's office, where what the user **asks for** her node to do is kept, **in progress** until it **ends**, and **how it ended**, in her words: **done**; **done already** (nothing changed, DOM-6); **cannot be done** (the kernel's business refused it); **the extension told how it ended** (no words of the kernel's: the screens tell it in the extension's own words, A, 22 Nov 2026; one of the extension's own events, an answer or none, whose meaning its texts tell, EXT-16); **no longer followed** (the screen stopped following before the end, DOM-3); **the extension cannot be reached** (**not active**, or **stopped** after two falls, EXT-15); **the app cannot do it** (a base event or a closing of `self`'s node: the app's fault, never hers). An activity is **carried out** by the office's effect, the library's queue (LIB-12): a fall is tried again, an activity that cannot be carried out is a **held letter**. **The drafts** (`user/drafts`) keep a text she started, by **what it is for** (the name of her first face, the name she gives a bond), until she **drops** it. **The choices** (`user/choices`) keep **the active face**, the face she acts as. **What an extension asks** is the notice of its activation: what its manifest declares, in her words, and **the flaws the kernel cannot fence** (EXT-7). A **held letter** is a dead letter as she is told it (the paper's word is never shown, D67), which she may **send again** or **put aside**; **what happened without a fact** is a trace entry as she is told it (F6). Rejected: `tasks` and `jobs` (the paper's word is _attività_, D76), `outbox` and `queue` for the office (the library's words, of every effect), `pending` (_in sospeso_: an activity is in progress, D74.2's _in corso_), `dead letter` in the user's words (D67), `session` for the choices (a technical word), `current face` (_volto attivo_ is the screens').

The screens' words (part 16.1, UI-1..UI-5, Claude, 21 Nov 2026): **the screens** (`ui`) are the screen model, a pure domain: each **screen** has its presentational state, **what happens** on it (her gestures, and what it comes to see: what it asked ended, a ticket ready, the camera), **a turn** (its state after, **what it asks** of her node, **where it goes**: nowhere, to a screen, back, **to the start**), **what it is given** to show and **what it shows**. **The way** is the screens she opened, the last shown. **The course** of what a screen asked is nothing asked, in progress, or ended with how. A **mark** is the kernel's mark of an extension at a place, holding its actions, slots and pages there. The names of the screens are provisional, as the words she reads (UI-5): **the first face**, **my contacts** (the home of stage 1), **a contact**, **the handshake in person**, **the handshake at a distance**, **an invitation**, **the extensions**, **an extension**, **a page**, **what happened**, **the server**, and **the introduction** (MVP part 4, Claude, 25 Nov 2026: three **cards** turned one after the other, **read the first time** after her first face and before the server, which she may **skip**, and **read again** from the menu, which goes back where she was; UI-6). Rejected: `view` and `page` for a screen (a page is an extension point, EXT-16), `route` and `stack` for the way (the router's words), `action` for what a gesture asks (an action is an extension point), `chat` for the home (no chats before messaging).

The stack's words (step 17, STK-1..STK-6, Claude, 21 Nov 2026): **the stack** (`memory`, _la pila in memoria_) is the phones and the relay in one process, over q-'s oracle; **a phone in the stack** is a phone's composition root there, **on** while its app is open (its consumers run, it collects) and **off** otherwise. **The collector** (_il raccoglitore_) collects the mailboxes the node collects at the relay and hands each deposit not seen yet to the kernel's entry; it reads them through the **collect port**, as the post deposits through its mailbox port: the two **transports** between a phone and the relay. **The ticket shown** is a ready ticket with its secret, what a QR or a link carries. **The extensions follow the office**: the node loads an extension the office says active and unloads one it says no longer active. Rejected: `poller` and `fetcher` for the collector (the transport's words, not the node's), `cluster` and `network` for the stack (no network in one process), `online`/`offline` for a phone (a phone off may have a network: its app is closed).

The words of the relay's HTTP client (step 20, WEB-6, WEB-7, Claude, 21 Nov 2026): **the relay's HTTP client** (_il client HTTP del relay_) carries a phone's two transports, the mailbox port and the collect port, over the relay's HTTP interface. A request that gets **no answer** is the **request lost** (it never reached the relay), the **answer lost** (it broke on its way back: the relay may have kept the envelope), or the relay unheard for **the longest wait** (_l'attesa più lunga_), 30 seconds for an answer to begin or for its next bytes; an **abort** (_un'interruzione_) is a request the page or the runtime let go, which the client signals. **The relay served** (_il relay servito_) is the relay's configurator on SQLite with its interface on a port of the machine, as the tests open and close it. Rejected: `timeout` (the paper's word for a transient failure, rejected for the post already), `silence` (a node's, F8), `retry` for the client (it tries nothing again: the post and the collector do), `fetcher` (rejected for the collector already).

The words of the relay's main (step 24, REL-1..REL-6, Claude, 25 Nov 2026): **the relay's main** (_il main del relay_) starts the relay on a machine from **the relay's settings** (_le impostazioni del relay_), read from its environment, and stops it at a signal; **the machine** (_la macchina_) is what of the computer the relay runs on a test gives in its place: its volumes, its monotonic time. **The room on the disk** (_lo spazio sul disco_) is **enough** or **too little** (_abbastanza_, _troppo poco_): too little when the volume's free space is below **the free space kept** (_lo spazio libero tenuto_); then **the relay is full** (_il relay è pieno_), the transport's refusal of a deposit. **What the relay says of itself** (_ciò che il relay dice di sé_) is its version, its commit and the source of that commit; **the relay's build** (_la build del relay_) is the one module Node runs. Rejected: `quota` (a share given to someone: the relay gives none, A, 25 Nov 2026), `config` and `env` for the settings (the tools' words), `health` for `GET /` (it says what the relay is, not how it feels), `binary` and `artifact` for the build (a module, run by Node).

The words of the relay's deploy (step 24, part 2, REL-7, Claude, 25 Nov 2026): **a release** (_una release_) is a commit of the relay built and copied to its machine; **the current release** (_la release corrente_) is the one the relay runs, **the release before** (_la release precedente_) the one it ran until the last **deploy** (_il deploy_); **the rollback** (_il ritorno alla precedente_) makes the release before current again, by itself when a new release does not answer with its commit. **The public configuration** (_la configurazione pubblica_, `docs/relay.md`) is what the relay's operator declares of it. Rejected: `version` for a release (the version is what `GET /` says, a release is where it lives), `revert` (git's word, for a commit).

The words of the node's storage (part 21.1, WEB-8..WEB-13, Claude, 21 Nov 2026): **the node's database** (_il database del nodo_) is a node's one IndexedDB database, with a store per business domain of the kernel, one per extension's partition, and **the node's store** (_l'archivio del nodo_), which keeps the node's own records: the writer's term, the counters of its one order, the consumers' state. Of the tabs that open the node, one is **the writer** (_lo scrittore_), the one that holds the browser's lock of the writer; each time a tab becomes it, it writes a new **writer's term** (_il mandato dello scrittore_), which every commit reads, so that a tab whose lock was taken writes nothing more. **Use here** (_usa qui_) is the gesture by which another tab takes the writer. **The storage kept** (_la memoria conservata_) is what the browser answers when the writer asks it to keep the node's storage: **persistent** (_persistente_), **best effort** (_a discrezione_, the browser may clear it) or **not told** (_non detto_). Rejected: `leader` (an election, which this is not), `lease` (a lock that lasts a time, which the browser's does not), `epoch` for the term (a word of time, where the term counts turns). Part 21.2 (WEB-14..WEB-16, Claude, 21 Nov 2026): **a domain's record** (_un record del dominio_) is a value of a business domain in its store, under its **kind** (_il suo tipo_: `face`, `bond`, `request`…) and its ids, one kind for each set of values the domain keeps; **a phone's storage** (_ciò che il telefono tiene_) is what a phone keeps as its composition root wires it: each kernel domain's ports, the node's log, the outbox, the consumers' state, the versions, the partitions; the oracle's cell is one, the node's database another. Rejected: `entity` and `table` for a kind (the paper's core defines no entities, LIB-14; a store is no table), `repository` and `persistence` for the phone's storage (a pattern's word and a technology's, where this names what the phone keeps).

The words of the screens' rendering (part 22.1, WEB-18..WEB-21, Claude, 21 Nov 2026): **her node** (_il suo nodo_), as the screens see it, is what they read of it in their words, what a gesture asks of it, and **when it changes** (_quando cambia_), which the screens follow to read it again; **the device** (_il dispositivo_) gives the screens an id, the time and the camera at a gesture; **the app** (_l'app_), what the rendering gives every screen: her node, the device, the words every screen shares, the count of her node's changes, and the two hands of a turn, what it asks and where it goes; **the words of a screen** (_le parole di uno schermo_), its texts in one language. What the words of the kernel mean (Alessandro, 19 Nov 2026; their rules come with the extensions' step of [`plan.md`](plan.md)):

- The **kernel** is what every node of Cumino has: faces, bonds, letters, relays, and the entry of a request (auth and the personas). Every other feature is an **extension**, which brings its domains and adds leaves to the personas' trees; two nodes speak through an extension when one has the side that asks and the other the side that serves.
- An extension's **manifest** declares what it sees of the kernel and of the other extensions, what it touches, the leaves it adds to which personas and the use cases it offers to other extensions; the kernel enforces it. An extension the app ships is **integrated** (Alessandro, 24 Nov 2026): in the kernel's process, it speaks to the kernel by messages only, its storage a partition the kernel gives it, and the manifest informs, it does not fence (integrated means trusted, told the user); **isolated**, for an extension installed from elsewhere, is a future mode.
- An **extension point** is where the kernel lets an extension in: a page, a slot, an action; a hook, a programmatic one, only where a case shows it is needed.
- A **request** is a letter that invokes a use case of a face bonded to the sender. A **response** is the remote form of the leaf's projection: a letter back that names the request it answers, received as the return of that request; a response asks for no response. Whether a feature answers by a response or by a request of its own is the feature's choice.
- A node **stays silent** when it has not the extension invoked, or does not offer it to that bond, and the two cases look the same from outside; the node that asked waits for a time, then says **no response**.
- The **extensions in common** of two known faces are read through a use case of the kernel, offered to the persona `known`.
- **Ping pong** is the first extension, **preinstalled** and off until the user activates it: a ping is a request, the pong its response.

Their rules are F7, F8 and EXT-1..EXT-8 of [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) (step 12's design, Claude, 20 Nov 2026), with these words:

- The **boundary** of an extension is the messages between it and the kernel: an **invocation** of one of its leaves in, the leaf's events out, its calls to the kernel's ports (EXT-2); and its life (ready, started, stopped), its parts' messages, and its notices, its dead letters and trace shown in its mark.
- An extension is **installed** when the node has it (in stage 1 the app's composition); **activated** and **deactivated** by the user, who accepts its manifest; **offered to a bond** while active, to every bond but those the user **withdraws the offer** from (EXT-7).
- A silence, an undeclared call and a response not awaited go to the **kernel's trace**, the trace of its entry, beside the trees' (EXT-6), with why: inside the node nothing happens in silence (F6).
- The **kernel's entry** takes each envelope collected at a mailbox of mine: opened by the face of mine at that mailbox, or traced as an envelope not opened; a request goes to the persona it names, to the kernel or, routed, to an extension, or the node stays silent; a response goes to the request it names, or is traced as not awaited. A request's response is **answered on the bond** of the `Caller`'s key: a request from a face with no bond gets none.
- The **port of requests** is the kernel's: it sends a request on a bond and answers with its response, or `no_response` after the time the asker set; the response comes back as the **return** of the request, its effect's return use case (F7). Its answer is closed: the response, `no_response`, the bond gone, or refused (the call undeclared, the extension not active or not offered to that bond, the input too large for a letter); it keeps each answer under the request's **identity**, which the kernel composes from the effect and the entry's id (F7, EXT-2). A request in flight is **called off** when the user deactivates its extension or withdraws its offer from the bond: sent again no more, refused, as a new one would be (F7, EXT-7). For the kernel's own requests (the extensions in common, EXT-8) it refuses nothing: the kernel declares them itself, it is never inactive, and an offer is an extension's (part 13.3).

How an extension is built and loaded (part 12.2, EXT-9..EXT-16):

- An extension is a **package** of its own (`packages/extensions/ping_pong/`), whose root is its tree; it imports its library, `zod` and the **`boundary`** package, the kernel's contract with its extensions (EXT-9). The **boundary version** is that contract's, in a window the kernel speaks.
- Its **manifest** is its declaration (`manifest.ts`) and its surface, which the build derives from its tree (EXT-10).
- **Integrated**, the default of the extensions the app ships, it runs in the kernel's process, loaded only when active, and can reach all that Cumino reaches, a flaw declared to the user; **isolated**, in a realm that reaches nothing of the node but the boundary, is a future mode; what runs isolated, **whole or in part**, comes with its case (EXT-13). Its storage is a **partition** of the node's, which only it reads and writes (EXT-14).
- An extension is **loaded** only while it is active, says it is **ready**, and its consumers are **started** and **stopped** by the kernel (EXT-15).
- An extension point sits in a **place** of the kernel's screens (on a bond, in the extensions); an action is data the kernel draws, a slot or a page a **part** of the extension's (a component in the kernel's mark when one comes; until 24 Nov 2026 a **frame**); each in the kernel's **mark** of its extension, its name and a border (EXT-16); a slot may take the whole screen, its subject kept, the extension's name in the corner (A, 24 Nov 2026; WEB-30).

Two names that collide in Italian: the paper's _busta_ is the **event envelope**; q-'s _busta_ (what the relay sees) is the **envelope**. The paper's _nucleo_ is the **nucleus**; the _core_ is the pure part of a domain; Cumino's _nucleo_ is the **kernel**. The paper's _frame_ is a frame of the request's stack; q-'s _riquadro_, a slot as the user reads it, is a **part** in the code (a frame until 24 Nov 2026), so that _frame_ never says the kernel's _cornice_, the **mark** (EXT-4, EXT-16).

The paper's _soggetto_ has two senses: what a query speaks of (D50.1, D64.1), and on whose behalf one acts (D87.1). q- keeps only the first: **subject** is always a query's, a view named by the business, where the paper's is an aggregate (V12). A **candidate** is an instance of the subject that a selection looks at; an **entry** is what the query returns of a candidate it keeps (its fields). Both are q-'s words, not the paper's.
