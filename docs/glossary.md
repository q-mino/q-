# Glossary

The code is in English; the app speaks Italian to its users (`packages/phone/src/texts/`), and the project's decisions are written in Italian. This table keeps the two vocabularies aligned. A new domain term enters here before it enters the code.

The terms of the architecture paper, which is in Italian, are in [Architecture terms](#architecture-terms); [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) uses them.

The relay's words (part 8.1, MBX-1..MBX-4, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): a **deposit** is an envelope left on a deposit address D, with the time the relay received it; the relay **discards** it seven days after, collected or not. Whoever deposits is a **depositor**, anyone; whoever shows the collect secret R of the mailbox at D = hash(R) is its **holder**, and collects. The relay's domain is `mailbox/deposits`, its personas `desk/depositor` and `desk/holder`. Rejected: `box` (a second word for the mailbox), `sender` (the relay knows no sender, F2, and a depositor need not be one). A day after its discard the relay **forgets** a deposit (part 19, MBX-8, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): it keeps nothing of it, neither its row nor its facts, so that a retry of its deposit is a new deposit; rejected: _expire_ (said of a ticket, part 11.2), _purge_ (an operator's act, not the relay's own rule).

The secrets' words (part 9.1, SEA-1..SEA-7, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): the **safe** is the domain of `seals` that keeps every secret of the node: the **seeds** (32 random bytes, with their **seed scheme**, `slip10-flat`, and the **next index** a face of the seed will take), the **faces** (each at its **index** under its seed, `m/i'`, with its **private key**, its **chain code** and its **public key**, the only one that leaves) and the **mailbox keys** (a collect secret R with its deposit address D, as the key and the address of a post-office box). A **content** is what `post` gives to sign or to seal, bytes opaque to the safe; a **sealed content** is one sealed for a public key; a signature **holds** when it is the face's over that content. A content is **not sealed** for bytes that are no public key one can seal for (not a point of Ed25519's group, or a point of small order: libsodium refuses them, part 18), as a sealed content is **not opened** by a face it was not sealed for. The **primitives port** is the safe's machine: the randomness, HMAC-SHA512, Ed25519, the sealed box, BLAKE2b (libsodium, step 18). Rejected: `keys` (a second word for the faces' keys and the mailboxes'), `keyring`, `vault` (Alessandro's notes are the vault).

The bonds' words (part 11.1, BND-1..BND-6, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): the **address book** is the domain of `bonds` that keeps the node's user's **faces** and **bonds**, as an address book keeps one's own cards and one's contacts. A face has its **name** (the one it is **introduced as** to whoever it is shown), its **position** in the user's tree of faces (**at the top**, or **under a face**: data of the user's, never of the keys) and its public key, once the safe made it. A bond is one of my faces with **their face** (its key and the name it is introduced as) and **their mailbox** (its deposit address and its **relays**, the **relay addresses** where it is deposited on), **my mailbox** for them (the key's reference in the safe, its D and its relays), **the name I give** them, and the node's annotations: the **handshake**, **in person** or **at a distance**, and when; the **confirmation**, **awaited** until a letter of theirs comes on the bond ("does she have you?"), then **received**. A bond is **kept** in the list until the user **removes** it. Rejected: `contacts` (the screens' word, not the domain's: a bond is the relation, not the person), `peer` in the components (the architecture's word for a remote node), `directory` (an emergence of the network's, a shared index).

The words of the handshake (part 11.2, BND-7..BND-12, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): a bond begins as a **ticket**, asked for by the user for one of her faces: **being prepared** while the safe draws the key of the bond's mailbox, **ready** once it has its D, shown in a QR or sent after `#` in a link, then **used**, when the bond is kept, or **expired**, 48 hours after it was asked for. The **ticket's secret** is the face's signature over the ticket's mailbox address: whoever has the ticket has it, no relay can forge it. To **open their ticket** is to bond with it, presenting a ticket of mine: **my introduction** goes to their ticket's mailbox; **their introduction** comes to mine. **Crossed tickets** (the **crossed QR**) are two tickets each side opened of the other's, within two minutes: the handshake **in person**. The **first face** is made at the phone's **first start**. **The mailboxes I collect** are my ready tickets' and my kept bonds'. Rejected: `invitation` (a ticket invites no one in particular, and is shown to whoever is in front), `invitee` for the persona (the paper's `unknown`, PER-11), `pass`, `code` for the secret (it is a signature, not a number to type).

The words of the browser's I/O (part 23, WEB-1..WEB-5, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **the ticket's link** is the address of the phone's page with a ready ticket after `#`, so that it never reaches the server that serves the page; **the ticket's QR** draws that link, so that a camera outside Cumino opens it too. To **scan** is to look at QRs with the **camera**, which is **on**, or **refused** by the user or the browser, **missing**, or **busy** (another app has it). To **save a file** gives the user a file to keep (an export); to **pick a file** takes one the user chooses (an import), or nothing. The **device's clock** is the instant its runtime reads, told in the zone the configuration gives; an **id drawn at random** is 16 bytes of the device's cryptographic randomness, in base64url, for the ids a node names before the first attempt (DOM-12). Rejected: `invitation link` (a ticket invites no one in particular, as above), `upload` and `download` (no server: the file stays on the device), `UUID` for the ids (a second alphabet beside base64url's).

The post's words (part 10.1, PST-1..PST-9, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): a **letter** is a request or a response (F7), with its **declared time**, the sender's claim (PER-13); the **signed content** is what the sender's face signs, the letter with the deposit address it is for, so that a letter **re-addressed** to another mailbox does not hold there; an **envelope** is the sealed letter with its format, what a relay keeps; an **opened envelope** gives the letter and its `Caller`, or why not. **The requests** (`post/requests`) are the port of requests' record: each one **sent**, **sent again** while it waits within **its time**, and **closed**, **answered** by its response or with **no response**. **The post's machine** is what the safe does for the post, with a face of the node's user: sign, seal, open, check a signature, hash (GR-5). The **mailbox port** is how the post deposits an envelope at a relay, which **keeps** it, keeps one of that deposit **already** (an earlier attempt), refuses it, or does not answer; each deposit has its **operation**, the hash of what is deposited (a sending, a response) and its D, the same for the same deposit tried again (part 10.2, PST-10, PST-11). Rejected: `outbox` for the requests (the library's word, of every effect), `timeout` (the paper's, a transient failure; here a meaning, V18), `registered mail` (a second word for a request).

The words of auth and the personas (part 13.1, PER-22..PER-24, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **the acquaintances** (`auth/acquaintances`, _le conoscenze_) is auth's office: the register of whom the node knows, at which of its mailboxes, and what it offers them, read from the address book and the office of the extensions by its adapters (GR-4), never decided there (PER-9). Its key is **a face at my mailbox**: the key that signed a letter and the mailbox of mine the letter came to, both verified by `post` (F2, PER-13). **The bond of a face** at my mailbox is the bond kept there whose face is that key, with the face of mine it belongs to; **the ticket a face presents** at my mailbox is a ready ticket of mine there, or the bond kept there with that face (the crossed QR), what an introduction is for. Who asks (the `Caller`, PER-4) is **the user** of the node, from the app, **a face**, by the key whose signature holds, or **anonymous**. To **introduce myself** is the leaf of `unknown`, the introduction as the face that presents a ticket says it. Rejected: `contacts` and `bonds` for auth's office (the address book's words, GR-4: auth keeps its own language), `register` (a verb in the code), `peers` (the architecture's word for a remote node, not a relation).

The words of ping pong (part 14.1, PNG-1..PNG-6, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **ping pong** (`ping_pong`) is the first extension, and **the pings** (`business/pings`, _i ping_) its office, which **sends a ping** to a bond, **asks** the bond's node **for a pong** (the request, F7), and **closes the ping** with what came back: **the pong came back**, **the ping had no response** within its time, or **the peer cannot answer** it (its node closed the request or refused it, or its office gave no pong); on the served side it **answers a ping** with a **pong**, which the leaf `ping` on `known` projects as its response. Each closing is a happening of its own (`a_pong_has_been_received`, `a_ping_has_had_no_response`, `a_ping_could_not_be_answered`, DOM-6). Rejected: `ping_pong` for the office (the extension's name, not what its office keeps), `echo` (a second word for the pong), `heartbeat` and `presence` (a ping tells that a bond's node answered once, nothing it keeps), `latency` (a measure, not a happening).

The user's words (part 15.1, USR-1..USR-8, Claude, 20 Nov 2026): **the activities** (`user/activities`, _le attività_) is the user domain's office, where what the user **asks for** her node to do is kept, **in progress** until it **ends**, and **how it ended**, in her words: **done**; **done already** (nothing changed, DOM-6); **cannot be done** (the kernel's business refused it); **the extension answered** (one of the extension's own events, whose meaning its texts tell, EXT-16); **no longer followed** (the screen stopped following before the end, DOM-3); **the extension cannot be reached** (**not active**, or **stopped** after two falls, EXT-15); **the app cannot do it** (a base event or a closing of `self`'s node: the app's fault, never hers). An activity is **carried out** by the office's effect, the library's queue (LIB-12): a fall is tried again, an activity that cannot be carried out is a **held letter**. **The drafts** (`user/drafts`) keep a text she started, by **what it is for** (the name of her first face, the name she gives a bond), until she **drops** it. **The choices** (`user/choices`) keep **the active face**, the face she acts as. **What an extension asks** is the notice of its activation: what its manifest declares, in her words, and **the flaws the kernel cannot fence** (EXT-7). A **held letter** is a dead letter as she is told it (the paper's word is never shown, D67), which she may **send again** or **put aside**; **what happened without a fact** is a trace entry as she is told it (F6). Rejected: `tasks` and `jobs` (the paper's word is _attività_, D76), `outbox` and `queue` for the office (the library's words, of every effect), `pending` (_in sospeso_: an activity is in progress, D74.2's _in corso_), `dead letter` in the user's words (D67), `session` for the choices (a technical word), `current face` (_volto attivo_ is the screens').

Choices: `post`, not `mail` (it suggests email); `ticket` for the biglietto (single use, with an expiry); `bonds`, not `links` (ambiguous in code); `kernel` for Cumino's nucleo, not `nucleus` (the paper's, of the library) nor `core` (the pure part of a domain) (Alessandro, 19 Nov 2026). `conversations` (`conversazioni`) is no longer a domain of the kernel: messaging is the first extension after stage 1 (Alessandro, 19 Nov 2026).

What the words of the kernel mean (Alessandro, 19 Nov 2026; their rules come with the extensions' step of [`plan.md`](plan.md)):

- The **kernel** is what every node of Cumino has: faces, bonds, letters, relays, and the entry of a request (auth and the personas). Every other feature is an **extension**, which brings its domains and adds leaves to the personas' trees; two nodes speak through an extension when one has the side that asks and the other the side that serves.
- An extension's **manifest** declares what it sees of the kernel and of the other extensions, what it touches, the leaves it adds to which personas and the use cases it offers to other extensions; the kernel enforces it. An extension is **isolated** by default (it speaks to the kernel by messages only, its storage a partition the kernel gives it); one **in process** is a declared choice of trust, which the user accepts informed of its risks, whole or in part.
- An **extension point** is where the kernel lets an extension in: a page, a slot, an action; a hook, a programmatic one, only where a case shows it is needed.
- A **request** is a letter that invokes a use case of a face bonded to the sender. A **response** is the remote form of the leaf's projection: a letter back that names the request it answers, received as the return of that request; a response asks for no response. Whether a feature answers by a response or by a request of its own is the feature's choice.
- A node **stays silent** when it has not the extension invoked, or does not offer it to that bond, and the two cases look the same from outside; the node that asked waits for a time, then says **no response**.
- The **extensions in common** of two known faces are read through a use case of the kernel, offered to the persona `known`.
- **Ping pong** is the first extension, **preinstalled** and off until the user activates it: a ping is a request, the pong its response.

Their rules are F7, F8 and EXT-1..EXT-8 of [`architecture.md`](architecture.md) (step 12's design, Claude, 20 Nov 2026), with these words:

- The **boundary** of an extension is the messages between it and the kernel: an **invocation** of one of its leaves in, the leaf's events out, its calls to the kernel's ports (EXT-2); and its life (ready, started, stopped), its frames' messages, and its notices, its dead letters and trace shown in its mark.
- An extension is **installed** when the node has it (in stage 1 the app's composition); **activated** and **deactivated** by the user, who accepts its manifest; **offered to a bond** while active, to every bond but those the user **withdraws the offer** from (EXT-7).
- A silence, an undeclared call and a response not awaited go to the **kernel's trace**, the trace of its entry, beside the trees' (EXT-6), with why: inside the node nothing happens in silence (F6).
- The **kernel's entry** takes each envelope collected at a mailbox of mine: opened by the face of mine at that mailbox, or traced as an envelope not opened; a request goes to the persona it names, to the kernel or, routed, to an extension, or the node stays silent; a response goes to the request it names, or is traced as not awaited. A request's response is **answered on the bond** of the `Caller`'s key: a request from a face with no bond gets none.
- The **port of requests** is the kernel's: it sends a request on a bond and answers with its response, or `no_response` after the time the asker set; the response comes back as the **return** of the request, its effect's return use case (F7). Its answer is closed: the response, `no_response`, the bond gone, or refused (the call undeclared, the extension not active or not offered to that bond); it keeps each answer under the request's **identity**, which the kernel composes from the effect and the entry's id (F7, EXT-2). For the kernel's own requests (the extensions in common, EXT-8) it refuses nothing: the kernel declares them itself, it is never inactive, and an offer is an extension's (part 13.3).

How an extension is built and loaded (part 12.2, EXT-9..EXT-16):

- An extension is a **package** of its own (`packages/extensions/ping_pong/`), whose root is its tree; it imports its library, `zod` and the **`boundary`** package, the kernel's contract with its extensions (EXT-9). The **boundary version** is that contract's, in a window the kernel speaks.
- Its **manifest** is its declaration (`manifest.ts`) and its surface, which the build derives from its tree (EXT-10).
- **Isolated**, it runs in a realm that reaches nothing of the node but the boundary, and the network only as far as the browser leaves it open, a limit declared to the user; **in process**, a choice of trust the user accepts informed; what runs in process, **whole or in part**, comes with its case (EXT-13). Its storage is a **partition** of the node's, which only it reads and writes (EXT-14).
- An extension is **loaded** only while it is active, says it is **ready**, and its consumers are **started** and **stopped** by the kernel (EXT-15).
- An extension point sits in a **place** of the kernel's screens (on a bond, in the extensions); an action is data the kernel draws, a slot or a page a **frame** the kernel places, with the extension's document in it; each in the kernel's **mark** of its extension, its name and a border (EXT-16).

Two names that collide in Italian: the paper's _busta_ is the **event envelope**; q-'s _busta_ (what the relay sees) is the **envelope**. The paper's _nucleo_ is the **nucleus**; the _core_ is the pure part of a domain; Cumino's _nucleo_ is the **kernel**. The paper's _frame_ is a frame of the request's stack; q-'s _riquadro_ is the **frame** of an extension point (EXT-16).

The paper's _soggetto_ has two senses: what a query speaks of (D50.1, D64.1), and on whose behalf one acts (D87.1). q- keeps only the first: **subject** is always a query's, a view named by the business, where the paper's is an aggregate (V12). A **candidate** is an instance of the subject that a selection looks at; an **entry** is what the query returns of a candidate it keeps (its fields). Both are q-'s words, not the paper's.
